## How to Measure Your Ring Size

### Method 1: Use a ring that already fits

1. Open the [online ring size calculator](https://freeringsizecalculator.com/).
2. Calibrate your screen, ideally by matching the on-screen outline to a standard bank card.
3. Place a ring that fits the intended finger against the on-screen circle.
4. Adjust the circle to align with the **inside edge** of the ring, not the outer edge of its band.
5. Review the estimated size and approximate international equivalents.

You can also measure the ring's **inner diameter** with a physical ruler or caliper and enter the result directly in millimeters.

### Method 2: Measure your finger with paper

1. Wrap a narrow, non-stretch strip of paper around the finger without squeezing.
2. Mark where the paper overlaps, then flatten the strip.
3. Measure the marked length with a ruler: this is your finger's **circumference**.
4. Enter the measurement into the [finger circumference calculator](https://freeringsizecalculator.com/measure-finger).
5. Repeat the measurement and check that the chosen ring can pass over your knuckle.

### Method 3: Convert a ring size you know

If you already know your size in one country's system, use the [ring size converter](https://freeringsizecalculator.com/ring-size-converter) to find the closest supported equivalent in other systems. Because sizing conventions differ, converted sizes are **approximate**, not guaranteed matches.

## Measurement Accuracy and Important Notes

Ring sizing tools provide an **estimate rather than a guaranteed fit**. For better results:

- Calibrate your screen before using the on-screen ring tool, and recheck after zoom or display changes.
- For a printed ring sizer, choose **100% / Actual size**, disable *Fit to page*, and check that the printed reference line is exactly **50 mm**.
- Measure the finger you intend to wear the ring on, preferably more than once at a comfortable room temperature.
- Consider knuckle size, band width, finger swelling, and the retailer's own sizing chart.
- Use a professional jeweler for a final fitting, especially for expensive or non-resizable rings.
- For Oura and other smart rings, use the manufacturer's model-specific sizing kit instead of standard jewelry sizing.
